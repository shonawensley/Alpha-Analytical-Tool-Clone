• You’re not crazy — that framing is actually the right “engineering target”, with one important caveat.

  What you’re describing (and why it’s good)

  - You’re shifting from “must be profitable” to “can we consistently isolate the winner into a smaller search space,” i.e. a coverage / hit-rate-at-budget problem.
  - That’s exactly how to evaluate a complex signal stack without prematurely arguing about bet sizing or “guaranteed profit” in a near‑random environment.

  The caveat (so it stays honest and measurable)

  - “Hit rate” by itself can be faked by just outputting more candidates.
  - So every claim must be tied to a budget (how many combos you’d actually play) and compared to a baseline.

  The clean way to formalize what you mean

  - Define, per state+variant+draw: a ranked list of candidates (or a candidate set).
  - Measure:
      - Lift vs random: compare Top-K hit rate to K / 1000 (Pick3 has 1000 combos) so you’re measuring “how much better than chance”.
      - A “coverage curve”: hit rate as K increases (K=5,10,20,50,…). This tells you how quickly your system “homes in”.

  How “profitability” fits without forcing it

  - Once you have Top-K hit rate, profit is just math for a chosen wager type (cost per ticket × K vs payout).
  - So you can absolutely stay in “hit-rate mastery” mode now, and only later translate it into EV/bet policies.

  Why your sharepack + templates approach is aligned with this

  - Sharepacks freeze the world (inputs + tool outputs) so your hit-rate measurements don’t drift.
  - Winners artifacts give deterministic labels.
  - Templates are the human layer that captures why a hit/miss happened (pattern cluster behavior), which is what you’ll later compress into a Superbrain feature contract.
