def canon(s): return "".join(sorted(ch for ch in str(s) if ch.isdigit()))

How to interpret the NJ/NY metrics once you run the checks

When you open *_metrics.json or the expander in the app:

compression_ratio = total_families / total_patterns

Lower is more selective. Big swings between NJ and NY can indicate much noisier input tables for one state.

avg_top_hot_density (mean hot density among top N families by score)

Helps you spot states where strong families aren’t supported by hot‑zone evidence. If this is unexpectedly low for one state, drill into top families and confirm hot_hits / hot_total in families.csv makes sense.

winner_family_ids and winner_family_best_rank

If winners exist, these are the V‑TRAC family IDs and their rank among families.

Look for winners ranking inside the top decile of families. If not, either the winner’s family is genuinely weak or your set/draw inputs are off.

best_straight_rank

First occurrence of a type=="straight" row in the score‑sorted table. If this is large but winners mapped well, straights might be underweighted—worth noting, not immediately a bug.
