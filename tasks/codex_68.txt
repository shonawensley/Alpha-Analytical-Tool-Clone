• Yes — what you just outlined is the correct mental model, and I’m aligned with it.

  1) The day‑ahead rule (timeframe)

  - The Excel/history workbook date (D) represents “the world as of the end of D” (tables built from draws up to that point).
  - For a real predictive system, the thing you’re trying to predict is the next outcomes after that world snapshot, i.e. results date (D+1).
  - So for backtesting/master validation, we intentionally do: tables from D → evaluate against winners from D+1. That’s the only way to honestly simulate “what would we have seen before
    the winners existed?”

  In our current example, the sharepack folder sharepacks/2025-06-21/… is effectively the results date, and the corresponding history workbook would be ~2025-06-20 (day before). That’s
  consistent with your intent.

  2) Brain outputs vs Winners outputs (the split you’re describing)
  You’re also correct here:

  - Brain outputs (analysis outputs) are what the tool produces without knowing the winners (candidates, scores, signals).
  - Winners outputs are a separate, later step: once winners are known, we “grade” the brain outputs (4‑criteria hit tagging, ranks, etc.).

  This is why centralizing winners logging is logical: it’s a shared “grading layer” that can be applied to multiple tools’ brain outputs.

  3) “Does the system need to read the outputs twice?”
  You’re not crazy — and the answer is:

  - For the final app, the superbrain won’t “read the report files to think.” It will compute from data directly.
  - But during master validation, the outputs are the inspection surface + audit trail:
      - They let us see what the code is doing (scoring/features/evidence).
      - They give us a stable “contract” we can later simplify.
      - They let a second analyst (or future session) review without rerunning everything.

  So the outputs aren’t “redundant”; they’re the bridge from “code did something” → “we can understand it, measure it, and redesign it safely.”

  That analogy is spot on. The work we did (summarizers + run report) is specifically to support that:
