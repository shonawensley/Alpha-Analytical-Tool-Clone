# AAT9 Analysis Arena — How To Read Fresh-Window Results

Purpose:

- give a fixed reading order for fresh-window interpretation
- stop layer-mixing between arena truth, ranking, control-arm realization, and translator opportunity
- make fresh windows comparable to the canonical comparison windows

Use this after:

- `fresh-window-readiness`
- `ANALYSIS_ARENA__CYCLE__STAGE3_FRESH_WINDOW_DECISION_READINESS.md` when a Stage 3 package exists
- `pre`
- `post`
- `window-close`
- `window-decay-close` when tail results coverage exists

## 1. Verdict Block

Write or think in this order every time:

- Arena Truth verdict:
- Brain 2 Prioritization verdict:
- Control Arm verdict:
- Translator Opportunity verdict:

Only after those four should you ask:

- what does the decay/carryover companion say?
- what does the Stage 3 replay/restraint layer allow or block?
- what does the Stage 4C shadow translator prototype keep separated before any rewrite?
- what policy or budget implications seem worth future research?

## 2. Baselines

Main teaching baseline:

- `WINDOW_2026-01-15_to_2026-01-22`

Secondary stability anchor:

- `WINDOW_2026-01-05_to_2026-01-09`

Rule:

- compare a fresh window against both
- do not let one long January block become the entire truth model

## 3. Reading Order

### Step 1 — Arena Truth

Read first:

- pure finalist scorecard
- deep hit analysis
- relevant per-state validation shells if needed

Main questions:

- did Brain 1 preserve winner canonical / family / VTRAC truth?
- did the arena expose finalist/candidate-like pressure?
- are explicit arena box/exact signals present?

Good headline metrics:

- candidate-like event coverage
- VT-like finalist coverage
- explicit arena box / exact signal counts
- finalist-supported credited hits

Do not conclude here:

- that the branch is already a finished play engine
- that low Play Card conversion means Brain 1 failed

### Step 2 — Brain 2 Prioritization

Read next:

- board scoreboard / board verdict context
- tune-up diagnostics if you need cross-window context
- deep hit analysis ranking section

Main questions:

- did the board put hit-bearing states high enough?
- are credited hits concentrated in strong tiers or too mid-board?
- is one state being over-promoted repeatedly?

Good headline metrics:

- `top_primary_target`
- top-band concentration
- credited-hit rank distribution
- median credited-hit rank

Do not conclude here:

- that the control arm performed well or poorly
- that translator opportunity is absent

### Step 3 — Control Arm Realization

Read after truth and ranking:

- same-window legacy comparison
- performance gap report

Main questions:

- what did Candidate Universe keep?
- what did Play Card / the old budget stack express?
- where did the old downstream arm bottleneck realization?

Good headline metrics:

- Candidate Universe exact / box containment
- Play Card strict / any-box / any-exact
- budget-floor realization only as baseline diagnostics

Do not conclude here:

- that the arena itself only knew that much
- that old downstream compression defines the branch’s end state

### Step 4 — Translator Opportunity

Read last before policy/budget inference:

- translator-learning ledger
- performance gap opportunity rows
- frontier negative-control study when frontier cues are relevant

Main questions:

- where did the arena already know box or finalist territory that the old arm failed to express?
- did the teaching cohort expand?
- are the frontier cues here thresholded and corroborated, or just ambient?

Good headline metrics:

- `BOX_GAP` rows
- gap rows with `arena_box_signal`
- gap rows with `sandbox_box_seed`
- arena-explicit translator rows
- frontier threshold alignment

Do not conclude here:

- that a live translator already exists
- that raw frontier presence should be promoted

### Step 5 — Decay / Carryover Companion

Read after the main same-day package when the decay scorecard exists.

Main questions:

- did strong Arena-era state-day snapshots resolve later inside the configured horizon?
- how much of the lift was same-day carryforward versus true future-day decay?
- are delayed resolutions concentrated in top-ranked states or more diffuse cohorts?

Good headline metrics:

- same-day resolved
- horizon-resolved
- incremental decay lift
- resolution profile split
- right-censored rows

Do not conclude here:

- that same-day Arena truth should be restated using delayed resolutions
- that future-day decay should replace same-day grading

Main questions:

- which clusters survive leave-one-window-out confirmation?

### Step 7 — Fixture Replay Interpretation

Use Stage 4 as the controlled test bench:

- read mechanism-family scorecard before individual rows
- compare source A alone, source B alone, and overlap A+B
- separate `survived_as_boxed_translator_candidate` from support gates and watch/decay rows
- treat `survived_with_lineage_guardrail` as useful but not independently confirmed
- treat concentration-blocked rows as fragile fixtures, not promotion material
- mine negative-control summaries for future penalty/veto design

Do not conclude here:

- that Stage 4 changes live scoring
- that legacy locator names are future rule names
- that all replay candidates belong in one blended pool

### Step 8 — Stage 4B Primitive Readback

Use Stage 4B to collapse and interpret Stage 4:

- read primitive cluster registry before individual row excitement
- use the leave-one-window-out matrix as a stronger generalization check
- use the survivor/support/restraint casebook for concrete examples
- use the translator design queue to decide what a future read-only prototype should test
- keep duplicate-credit, concentration, support-only, VTRAC/decay, and negative-control lanes separate

Do not conclude here:

- that a cluster is a live rule
- that holdout confirmation bypasses prototype testing
- that support gates should become standalone candidates

### Step 9 — Stage 4C Shadow Translator Prototype

Use Stage 4C to translate the Stage 4B queue into strict prototype lanes:

- read the lane separation matrix before individual clusters
- read clean boxed and lineage-guarded candidate lanes as aggregate shadow expressions only
- read support-gate effects as context modifiers, not standalone candidates
- read decay/watch rows as carryforward/territory rows, not boxed spend rows
- read concentration and negative-control rows as restraint/retest surfaces
- use the holdout prototype scorecard to compare prototype modes without blending lanes

Do not conclude here:

- that Stage 4C changes live scoring
- that Stage 4C creates deployable candidate lists
- that support, decay, concentration, low-denominator, or negative-control lanes can bypass their guardrails

### Step 10 — Stage 5 Shadow Translator Fixture Evaluator

Use Stage 5 to evaluate what Stage 4C lanes actually do against completed state-day fixtures:

- read value completeness before interpreting value-level rows
- read prototype mode scorecard before casebook examples
- compare source A alone, source B alone, and overlap A+B through the ablation matrix
- compare candidate rows with support context versus candidate rows without support context
- compare kept versus removed rows in the restraint-effect audit
- check window and state stratification before treating any mode as durable
- use `PRO_44` compliance as the acceptance checklist for replay discipline

Do not conclude here:

- that Stage 5 changes live scoring
- that Stage 5 output is a deployable translator
- that casebook examples override denominator scorecards
- that incomplete samples can support exact value-level replay claims

### Step 11 — Stage 5 Readback Decision Memo

Use Stage 5 readback to turn evaluator outputs into explicit next-step gates:

- read mode decisions before proposing any translator/scoring specification
- treat `shadow_spec_only` as shadow-design permission, not live scoring permission
- keep support context, decay/watch context, restraint retests, and low-denominator watchlists in separate lanes
- use the next-action queue to decide whether the next task is narrowing, restraint calibration, macro-findings review, or shadow-spec drafting

Do not conclude here:

- that Stage 5 readback changes live scoring
- that a readback decision is a deployable rule
- that a Macro Findings Log entry should be made before the readback finding is evidence-led and clearly tagged as provisional, repeated, confirmed, deferred, or contradicted

### Step 12 — Stage 6A Shadow Translator Specification

Use Stage 6A to formalize the future shadow translator contract:

- read the lane contract before reading any future simulator output
- confirm the guardrail matrix blocks live scoring, support-only candidates, decay-to-box conversion, duplicate overlap credit, and blended lanes
- read simulation requirements as Stage 6B test definitions, not proof that the translator works
- read the acceptance checklist before proceeding to Stage 6B
- use the shadow spec queue to decide what the next read-only simulator must build

Do not conclude here:

- that Stage 6A changes live scoring
- that a Stage 6A lane is a deployable rule
- that Stage 6A proves an edge without Stage 6B replay

### Step 13 — Policy / Budget Implications

Only now ask:

- if the translator were stronger, would this look like `WATCH`, `PLAY`, or `SKIP`?
- does the future posture look boxed-first, straight-first, VT-box, or hybrid?
- what should later budget research study?

Keep this bounded:

- policy and budget are still downstream of truth, ranking, and translator learning

## 4. Quick Checklist

For every fresh window, answer these in order:

1. Did the arena trap truth?
2. Did Brain 2 prioritize that truth well?
3. Did the old control arm express that truth well or badly?
4. Is there a translator opportunity set here?
5. What does the decay/carryover companion add?
6. What does Stage 3 permit for replay, restraint, and casebook review?
7. What does Stage 4 fixture replay confirm, block, or keep as support/watch material?
8. What does Stage 4B collapse into primitive clusters and holdout-confirmed prototype queues?
9. What does Stage 4C separate into clean candidate-expression, lineage-dedup, support, decay/watch, restraint/retest, and low-denominator lanes?
10. What does Stage 5 prove or weaken when those lanes are replayed against completed state-day fixtures?
11. What does Stage 5 readback classify as shadow-spec seed, support-only, companion-only, retest/watchlist, or blocked?
12. What does Stage 6A formalize as the allowed shadow translator contract and Stage 6B simulator requirements?
13. Only then: what policy/budget questions are worth future research?

## 5. What Not To Do

- Do not use `B12/B24/B36` as the headline measure of branch quality.
- Do not use low Play Card conversion as proof Brain 1 failed.
- Do not use one strong January window as the only baseline.
- Do not promote raw feeder or raw VTRAC frontier presence into scoring.
- Do not mix ranking weakness and translator weakness into one vague complaint.
- Do not treat Stage 3 as a scoring rewrite. It is a replay/restraint/readiness layer.
- Do not treat Stage 4 as a scoring rewrite. It is the fixture test bench before later rewrite work.
- Do not assign duplicate credit to shared-lineage sources.
- Do not treat Stage 4B as a scoring rewrite. It is a primitive readback and holdout filter before prototype work.
- Do not treat Stage 4C as a scoring rewrite. It is a read-only shadow translator design package before any fixture-backed prototype evaluation.
- Do not blend Stage 4C lanes into a single pool.
- Do not treat Stage 5 as a scoring rewrite. It is the fixture-backed evaluator before any future translator/scoring specification.
- Do not use Stage 5 casebook examples as hand-picked rules.
- Do not treat Stage 5 readback as scoring permission. It is the interpretation gate before any shadow-only translator/scoring specification.
- Do not treat Stage 6A as scoring permission. It is the formal contract for a future read-only Stage 6B simulator.

## 6. Practical Close

The correct summary shape for a fresh window is:

- Arena Truth: what the arena trapped
- Brain 2 Prioritization: how well the board concentrated it
- Control Arm: what the old downstream arm expressed
- Translator Opportunity: what the future translator should learn from

That is the stable reading discipline for this branch.
