Here’s how the Positional Pressure (PP) tool we just designed fits cleanly with what you already ship, why it’s analytically useful (not just “more stuff”), and how you’ll use it day‑to‑day alongside Pairs, Sums, V‑Trac, Doubles, and Blackapple.

1) What PP actually produces (per state, per variant)

For Combined / Midday / Evening we compute, cache, and render:

Per‑position “Top‑3 due digits” with gaps and hazard/boost tags (e.g., Mirror, V‑pairs, RS).

A compact “position mask” like P1{7,0,4} · P2{8,6,7} · P3{7,0,3} so you can instantly see pressure.

A simple PP score per variant (0–6) plus per‑position scores (0–2) you can use as compounds.

Lightweight tags you already understand (Mirror, Root‑due, Pair‑cover, etc.) so this feels like the rest of Aux, not a foreign system.

All of that is computed from the same data/cleaned/*_draws.csv files the rest of Aux/Control Center use (no string‑table dependency). That keeps boundaries crisp and performance snappy.

2) How PP composes with the Aux features you have today

Pairs (red/blue)

What PP adds: Pairs tells you which unordered digit relationships are late; PP tells you where (P1/P2/P3) pressure is focused.

How to use together: If Pairs says 1–6 is red, and PP says P2 favors {1,6}, you’ve got a positional overlay that tightens boxed lists and straight plays.

V‑Trac Index

What PP adds: V‑Trac groups triple‑digit families; PP highlights digits most likely to appear in place.

How to use together: When a V‑Trac index is due, filter its boxed set by PP masks (e.g., “index 7 family” ∩ P1{7,0,4}) to thin the set to the likeliest straights.

Sums / Root sums

What PP adds: Sums are global constraints; PP is local, per position.

How to use together: Keep your sums thresholding exactly as is; PP just marks the top‑3 digits that also agree with the day’s sums/root‑due, so you don’t lose structure.

Doubles

What PP adds: If doubles are “on watch” (high gap across states), PP shows which digit is most likely to repeat and where (e.g., P2 pressure centering on 4 and P3 on 4 hints at x44/4x4).

Day‑to‑day: Your “Due Doubles” board remains the macro filter; PP is the micro placement cue per state/variant.

Blackapple (BA)

What PP adds: BA is your evidence stack and ranked candidate list; PP is a positional heat map that can sit beside BA as a separate, simple cue (no cross‑imports, no coupling).

How to use together: In Control Center add a tiny “PP Heat” column (0–3) by variant. On BA ALERT states, glance at PP: if BA was leaning on RS/Mirror and PP favors {7,0,4} on P1, you can sanity‑check or prioritize candidate straights faster. BA remains draws‑only and lives under modules/blackapple.py, surfaced primarily in Control Center; PP does the same, so ops stay uniform.

Safety & wiring: Both BA and PP read only the per‑state draws CSVs; neither touches the string‑tables pipeline. This is by design and matches your architecture guardrails (Launcher → src/app.py; Aux/Control Center → data/cleaned/*_draws.csv).

3) Where PP shows up (so it feels native)

Auxiliary Tools (state view): Under V‑Trac/Indicators/Pairs, you’ll see a “Positional Pressure” block with the 3 variant tabs. Each tab shows the three position columns, top‑3 digits with gaps/tags, and a concise PP score/caption.

Control Center (cross‑state view): Add a small “PP Heat” column per state (either combined only, or a variant toggle like you did for Doubles). Keep it minimal: a badge like E:2 • M:1 • C:3. This is not a candidate list—just a scanline that helps you triage which rows deserve a click.

This mirrors how BA is surfaced (table with small, readable fields; details behind an expander) and keeps the cross‑state page light.

4) Why PP is analytically valuable (and not “just more”)

Orthogonality: Pairs/V‑Trac/Sums talk about what digits relate to what; PP talks about where a digit is starving and likely to enter. Orthogonal signals are what you want for compounding—each adds something the other can’t infer.

Small, explainable features: Every number we show (gap, rank, tag) is transparent—easy to log, easy to learn from later. You asked for “compound scoring features”; PP gives you three tiny, stable scores (P1, P2, P3) that stack cleanly with your existing red/blue pairs, sums, and BA status.

Low cost, high reuse: PP runs in O(window) over one CSV, no joins or table reads. You can compute it daily for all states/variants without straining the app, and you can log it with the winner just like the rest of your Aux evidence.

5) How it plugs into Winners Logging and learning

On “post day,” write a single JSON/CSV per state/variant with:

PP P1/P2/P3 top‑3 lists + gaps + tags,

The PP score, and whether the winner matched P1/P2/P3 (and how many positions),

The other Aux hits you already log (Pairs red/blue hit, V‑Trac index hit, BA status).

Later, your learner doesn’t need to “understand PP” at all—it just sees features like PP_P1_hit=1, Pairs_Red_hit=1, BA_ALERT=1 and learns how that stack correlates with outcomes. That’s exactly how BA was documented to work at the Control Center (small overlapping triggers → ranked hints).

6) Reassurance on complexity & boundaries

Complex ≠ fragile when the boundaries are crisp. PP, like BA, is draws‑only and writes its own small artifact (or just caches in memory). It doesn’t alter combined tables or touch Stable/V‑Trac/Digit Reduction code paths. That matches your architecture rules and avoids the “path drift” headache we just fixed.

Self‑contained: You can add or tune PP without risking your perfected combined features. If a threshold ever feels too chatty, it’s a one‑line config knob—not a refactor.
